The Living Certificate

Proof, not promise.

Most food labels tell you how a farm is supposed to operate. The Living Certificate was created because we were more interested in what actually changes over time.

The problem

Why existing labels are not enough

Organic was once a meaningful distinction. Over time, as adoption increased and standards proliferated, it became harder for consumers to distinguish between the exceptional and the merely compliant. Regenerative agriculture is now heading towards the same problem.

Most food labels are built around process. If a farm follows a prescribed set of practices, it receives the label. The difficulty is that two farms can follow the same process and produce very different outcomes. One may improve biodiversity, build healthier soil and produce more nutrient dense food year after year, while another may show little measurable improvement at all.

The label cannot tell the difference. We think it should.

The Living Certificate

An outcomes based approach

An annual record of what is happening on each participating farm. Rather than rewarding a farm for following a process, it measures the outcomes that process produces, and records how they change over time.

Evidence

Make improvement visible

  • Better soil health is recorded

  • The claim moves only when the evidence does
What it measures

The four pillars

Soil Health

Healthy food begins with healthy soil, yet most food labels say remarkably little about the condition of the land itself. The Living Certificate tracks how each farm is improving the biological foundation everything else depends on.

Biodiversity

A working farm should support more life, not less. Independent ecological assessments help measure how habitats, species richness and biodiversity are changing over time.

Nutrient Density

Food should nourish. Independent laboratory analysis measures what is actually present in the beef, helping create one of the first datasets of its kind for British grass fed production.

Eating Quality

The beef should be as impressive on the plate as it is in the field. Independent assessment helps measure flavour, tenderness and overall eating quality, so quality is experienced as well as recorded.

How it works

Measured. Verified. Published.

Each participating farm documents its farming system throughout the year, including breed, grazing management, forage and finishing practices. Independent specialists then measure the relevant outcomes using recognised methodologies, after which the results are reviewed, verified and linked directly to the individual farm and production batch.

A record that grows alongside the farm itself, creating a transparent history of improvement that consumers can access directly from the pack.

Why it matters

For decades, many of the farmers producing the greatest long term value for society have received little recognition for doing so. Improvements in soil health, biodiversity and food quality rarely appear on a payslip, despite requiring years of work and stewardship. The Living Certificate exists because those improvements should count for something — not as a marketing claim, nor a theoretical exercise, but as a visible, measurable record of what has actually been achieved.
